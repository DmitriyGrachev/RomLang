# Curriculum depth audit — 28 October 2026

Scope: read-only review of the current course catalog, route and practice screens, `docs/romanian-site-design.md`, and `docs/briefs/course-depth-plan.md`. Learner: Ukrainian beginner in Bucharest/Militari, reading Russian and English, studying independently for 60–90 minutes on six days a week. This is a pedagogical audit, not a Romanian native-speaker edit or an assessment of the learner.

## What the learner sees versus what exists

- The route's six visible nodes are **one selected module**: four lessons, a checkpoint and a branching dialogue. A module selector exposes six modules at each level. The catalog actually has **24 core lessons and six modules per level; 96 lessons and 24 modules overall**. The route lacks a full level map, so the first view reasonably suggests a six-step course. In progress, lesson totals are dynamic, but the route doesn't explain their scale.
- The course is more than those 96 lesson cards: 16 separate life situations with preparation and exercises, layered theory supplements, A1.1 adaptive practice, B2 extended reading/writing/speaking tasks, dictionary and review. These are real supporting assets, but should be named separately from core lessons and cannot make a short core lesson into a long standalone session by arithmetic.
- Core lessons have eight objective items each, two of each format. A simple count of each lesson's longest `read` item gives median Romanian whitespace words of about **8 A1 / 15 A2 / 22 B1 / 25 B2**. This counts only core lesson passages, excluding life units and four extended B2 exam readings. It illustrates the need for longer varied input, especially B1/B2. Core explanations are frequently compact even where supplements add depth. A learner finishing the first four questions quickly has not spent a full 60–90 minute learning day.
- All core `ExerciseItem.skill` labels are grammar or reading, including some items whose `tag` says listening. There is no audio player, native recording, or spoken-response grading. The B2 readiness screen says this plainly and links official external samples. Avoid presenting text-only answers or listening-themed tags as listening progress.

## Judgment on the expansion plan

The plan to add six fully authored modules and 24 lessons per level (12 modules/48 lessons per level; 192 lessons total), with deeper explanations, pre-taught language, 12 original items per new lesson, cumulative checks and two dialogue variants, is a **substantial, appropriate expansion**. Preserving IDs/progress and deriving counts from content are important. It plausibly supports many months of **structured self-study**, especially with revisits and delayed review; 192 is not an educational threshold and a lesson need not equal a calendar day. The plan's word/item/passage ranges are useful editorial floors, not proof of task quality, session duration, skill attainment, or a B2 result.

The largest missing specification is **what the 24 new modules teach and what each learner can do afterward**. Without a level-by-level map, contributors can hit every numeric target while repeating old grammar, skipping everyday functions, putting unknown forms in exercises, or producing 96 parallel quiz packets. UBB's description separates communicative functions, grammatical/lexical resources, and receptive/productive text types across A1–B2; use it as a coverage reference, not a prescribed lesson order or certification rule. Its B1/B2 text-type lists include conversation/argument, letters, news and extended text, well beyond multiple-choice sentence selection. [UBB, *Descrierea minimală a limbii române*, pp. 9–26, 49–71, 71–92](https://video.elearning.ubbcluj.ro/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Descrierea-minimala-a-limbii-romane-12-IULIE-2016.pdf). ILR supplies A1–B2 sample tests and real audio for independent checks; it does not certify this course. [ILR, *Aplicații și Nivel prag*](https://www.ilr.ro/category/atestate/aplicatii-si-nivel-prag/).

## Prioritized bounded changes

### P0 — Set the content contract before mass authoring

1. **Write a 24-row expansion map, one row per new module**, before drafting lessons. Each row should name: prerequisite old/new material, one new or deepened grammar contrast, an actual communicative outcome, Bucharest-relevant lexical domain, input genre and approximate complexity, productive genre, which earlier material is retrieved, and the module's unseen transfer condition. Map each of four lessons to a different microstep. Explicitly flag overlap with existing core topics and the 16 life situations, so an apparent repeat is a deliberate harder use or new context. Require review against UBB's three axes: function, linguistic resource, text type.
2. **Give each level a visible scope and honest success criteria.** A1: introductions and immediate life tasks with repair/clarification; A2: connected everyday events, changed plans and problems; B1: sustained narrative, explanation, request/complaint and discussion of familiar issues; B2: extended argument, counterargument, register, source comparison and editing. These are suggested outcomes, not a CEFR award. In each module checkpoint, use previously unseen situation details and at least one cumulative productive task. Show a next review or transfer action after finishing a module, rather than implying one pass establishes mastery.
3. **Editorially validate meaning before volume.** Keep the existing specified uniqueness/answer checks, but require a human Romanian language review of inflections, idiom, register, alternatives and the explanation of each answer. Especially audit multiple valid word orders and plausible responses in branching dialogue. Track an explicit reviewer/status and correct or drop ambiguous scored items; automated validators cannot check this.

### P1 — Make a six-day study cycle genuinely usable

4. **Put a flexible repeatable weekly pattern beside the full-program map.** An example: four days of a lesson microstep with theory → construction → short input → own response; one day of delayed recall and a new-life scenario; one day of an unseen cumulative check, dialogue and error log. Let one difficult lesson span days and let a quick one share a day. Present the existing 60/75/90-minute settings as allocations to review, new grammar, reading/real listening, writing/speech and reflection, with a resume point; do not promise a fixed finish date. The current daily `8 questions` meter is a practice prompt, not a 60–90-minute plan.
5. **Broaden receptive material by genre and purpose.** The proposed passage lengths are useful, but each level also needs messages, signs/menus/forms, exchanges, narratives and later articles/arguments with gist, detail, inference and response tasks. At B1/B2, a few longer passages and four existing B2 readings are not a sustained reading diet. Add multiple authored texts across the new modules, with deliberate unknown-word handling and questions that do not disclose answers in preparation. Recycle a text's language in a later new text, not in an identical key.
6. **Make productive transfer repeatable.** The plan's one checkpoint prompt and mission variants should require a learner to produce materially new details and decide how to ask, clarify, negotiate or disagree, then revise after a checklist/model. Add a short independent written message or paragraph at appropriate modules, with rubrics and full models revealed after attempt; B1/B2 should include longer correspondence and reasoned writing. Self-check and a copy-to-ChatGPT dialogue can support practice but must be shown as such, not scored speech/writing ability.

### P1 — Close the input and feedback boundary honestly

7. **Specify a real-listening routine that respects “no TTS.”** Link selected legitimate natural recordings with appropriate-level task directions (first listen for gist, replay for details, note a useful phrase, compare with available transcript/answer if any), reserve other ILR tests unseen for periodic independent checks, and label text-only activities reading. If no curated recurring listening can ship, state on the full-program map that listening needs external material and give a concrete external practice route; do not count it as internally taught or measured.
8. **Give periodic four-skill checks, with evidence and limits.** At each level boundary and a few intermediate points: fresh text comprehension, authentic audio comprehension, independent writing and unrehearsed interaction. Record assisted versus unassisted work and delayed attempts separately; show what the app can score and where a person or external assessment is needed. ILR official sample tests can be held-out checks, with no copied keys in training. Site completion/XP should stay separate from proficiency claims.

### P2 — Localize and make the map navigable

9. **Show all modules per level, their four lessons, checks and dialogues**, plus true dynamic counts and a clear “you are viewing one module” label on the original six-node route. Include a next/previous sequence and direct theory/practice links. Name core lessons, optional life units, word drills and extended B2 work distinctly. Avoid counting planned or empty assets in a published overview.
10. **Use Dima's actual context carefully.** Prioritize transport, apartment/building issues, shops, appointments, work, forms and everyday politeness in fictional Bucharest/Militari scenes. Present Russian explanation and selective RU/EN comparisons without implying Russian nationality; recognize Ukrainian as the learner's identity and optional bridge. Vary voices, interlocutors, register and conditions so dialogue isn't memorizing one script. Keep invented prices, policies and services visibly fictional.

## Release claim and residual limitation

If all 96 new lessons and supporting assets are original, linked into review, editorially checked, and shown through a full map and a realistic routine, the site may accurately claim a **substantial A1–B2-oriented, grammar-first self-study curriculum with months of practice**. It should not claim that its 192 lessons certify B2, or that it is self-sufficient for listening and spontaneous interaction while it lacks recurring natural audio and evaluated speaking. Full competence depends on unseen receptive input, real interaction and independent feedback; communicate those needs inside the learner's plan rather than as a footnote.
